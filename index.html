The source with the largest recoveries returned the least on capital.

Large matters with a 40% referral split and longer durations can underperform smaller, faster ones. Asteric shows the trade-off by source, category, attorney, jurisdiction and year.

Return = net fee (attorney fee − referral fee) + expenses reimbursed from a favorable recovery. Expense-only capital = litigation expenses. Fully costed adds attorney hours × the firm's configured hourly cost ($175 in the demo). Figures come from Asteric's synthetic demonstration firm.

Built for information a firm cannot afford to mishandle.

Tenant isolation

Each firm's records are separated in the application and again by Postgres row-level security.

Two-factor authentication

Authenticator-app codes with single-use recovery codes, available to every user.

Role-based access

Owner, administrator, attorney, analyst and read-only roles control what each member can do.

Audit logging

An append-only log of sign-ins, predictions, outcomes, imports, exports and settings changes.

Immutable predictions

Recorded predictions cannot be edited or deleted; the database enforces it.

Export and deletion

Owners can export all data as CSV and schedule permanent deletion with a 30-day grace period.

Encrypted infrastructure

HTTPS everywhere, a managed database encrypted at rest, and encrypted two-factor secrets.

Human-confirmed imports

Uncertain mappings wait for a reviewer. Nothing is guessed; every imported value keeps its source.

Asteric has not yet completed a SOC 2 or similar third-party audit. Asteric measures a firm's own historical records; it does not provide legal advice.
